# PIXEL CUP '96

**Low-poly arcade football, in a pixel buffer, in your pocket.**

Five a side. Ninety seconds a half. One ball, two teams and a crowd that
will not shut up.

PIXEL CUP '96 is a progressive web app: install it, then play it with the
aeroplane mode on. It works offline, it keeps your records on your own
device, and it asks for nothing — no account, no server, no tracking.

---

## The picture

There is no WebGL in this game and no image files either. The pitch, the
players, the ball, the goal and the crowd are all **triangles drawn by
hand into a 320×180 byte buffer**, which the browser then scales up with
nearest-neighbour sampling.

Two decisions make that work:

- **Flat shading.** Every face has one colour, computed from its normal
  against a single light, and then quantised to a limited palette. That
  is what makes the geometry read as *low-poly* rather than as a small
  smooth render.
- **A real depth buffer.** The rasteriser tests 1/d against it, and 1/d is
  an exact affine function of the screen position across a plane — so
  interpolating it across a triangle is not an approximation. The pitch,
  the players and the goalposts share one buffer without any sorting.

The result is a faceted stadium that is genuinely made of pixels: the
stands are a speckle of one-pixel supporters, the keeper is a box with a
cube on top, and the ball is an icosahedron that spins.

## The game

| Mode | What it is |
|---|---|
| **Quick Match** | One match against the computer. Pick your side, pick your difficulty. |
| **Cup** | Eight teams, a knockout bracket, a trophy. Your tie is played; the others are simulated. Progress is saved, so the cup survives a closed tab. |
| **Penalty Shoot-out** | Five kicks each, then sudden death. Stop the sweeping marker, then stop the power meter. |

### Controls

| Action | Keyboard | On screen |
|---|---|---|
| Move | WASD / arrows | the stick on the left |
| Pass | Space / K | **PASS** |
| Shoot | J / X | **SHOOT** |
| Sprint | Shift | **RUN** |
| Switch player | Q | **SWAP** |
| Skill move | L | **SKILL** |
| Pause | Esc | the pause button, top right |

A gamepad works too. "Up" is always towards the opponent's goal,
because the camera always looks that way.

- **Pass** taps for a short pass and charges for a harder one; push the
  stick up for a lob.
- **Shoot** charges a power meter. Release to strike; push the stick
  sideways to bend it and up to lift it.
- **Skill** is a through ball when you have the ball and a sliding
  tackle when you do not.
- **Pause** opens a sheet with Resume, Restart and Quit, and the sound,
  music and vibration switches. Leaving the tab pauses the match by
  itself: nobody wants to come back to a match that ran on without them.

### The touches

- A goal slows the world down for a third of a second, flashes the
  screen, throws confetti, and gets a shout from the terraces.
- The commentary is in Italian, because the terraces always are:
  *"Che gola!"*, *"Che sprechi!"*, *"Attenzione al contropiede!"*,
  *"Palla alta!"*, *"Annullato!"*.
- The ball hits the post, the keeper dives and the crowd groans.
- The boost meter fills while you have the ball and pays for your
  sprinting.
- Every sound is synthesised in the browser. There is not one audio file
  in this folder: the crowd is filtered noise, the whistle is two
  detuned squares, and the music is a sixteen-step square-wave
  sequencer.

**One copy of the rules.** `src/core.js` has no DOM in it and is loaded
twice: once by the browser and once by the Node tests. The tests
therefore exercise the game you actually play, not a copy of it.

### The model

- The pitch is 56 × 36 units; the goals are nine wide and 3.2 high.
- The ball has weight, bounce, friction and spin, and a strike bends in
  the air.
- A player who has just won the ball has half a second to turn away from
  the tackle before anybody can take it back.
- A goalkeeper guesses where a shot is going, and he is often wrong —
  which is why a well-placed shot beats him.
- The computer presses the man with the ball, marks, holds its shape, and
  shoots from range. It is beatable, and it is not stupid.

### Tests

```bash
node pixel-cup-pwa/tests/sim.test.mjs    # 89 checks: rules, physics, cup, shoot-out, balance
node pixel-cup-pwa/tests/boot.test.mjs   # 62 checks: the whole game against a fake DOM
node pixel-cup-pwa/tests/preview.mjs /tmp/pc.png 26   # writes a PNG of a live frame
node pixel-cup-pwa/smoke.mjs             # the real page, in a real browser
node pixel-cup-pwa/tools/gen-og.mjs      # writes the social card
```

The balance is pinned by the tests as well: a scripted player of ordinary
ability is expected to score, the opposition is expected to score too,
and a match is expected to contain somewhere between one and a half and
seven goals. If a change to the physics moves those numbers, the change
is wrong.

## Accessibility

- Every menu is ordinary HTML: real buttons, real focus, readable by a
  screen reader.
- The score, the clock and the important events are announced through an
  `aria-live` region.
- The pitch itself is described by its `aria-label`.
- Nothing relies on colour alone.
- `prefers-reduced-motion` removes the shake and the slow motion.

## Privacy

Everything is local. The records, the settings and the cup in progress
live in this browser's `localStorage` and nowhere else. There is no
account, no server and no analytics. The shared arcade library records
the same anonymous, on-device run log as the other games in this
repository, and the STATS page can show it to you or export it.
