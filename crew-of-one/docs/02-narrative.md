# 02 — Narrative

## Tone

Dry, stylish, a little melancholy. A heist story told in short beats, not cutscenes. Think a watchmaker’s letter, a rainy city, and a job that was supposed to be the last one.

## Frame

You are a retired thief. Your partner — a watchmaker — built the ChronoSafe: a watch that rewinds thirty seconds and leaves an echo of the run behind. She is gone. The vaults are the jobs you never pulled together. Each chapter reveals a line from her letter.

## Chapter beats

### Chapter 1 — The Gallery
You test the watch on a small gallery job. The first echo holds a door. The letter begins: “If you’re reading this, you went back for the watch.”

### Chapter 2 — The Archive
The jobs get colder and more personal. The letter reveals she built the watch because she was the one who couldn’t be in two places at once.

### Chapter 3 — The Vault
The final job is the one you were both supposed to pull. The last line: “I couldn’t be there. So I left you a way to be everywhere at once.”

## Delivery

- One typed line between chapters.
- A stamp, a paper texture, a single piano/bass note.
- No voice acting. No long paragraphs.
- Skippable.

## Title

**CREW OF ONE** is the commercial title. The final vault is called **“After You”** — the polite phrase and the literal one. Your past selves go first.

## Writing rules

- Short sentences. Concrete nouns. No exposition dumps.
- The watch is a tool, not a magic system. Don’t over-explain it.
- The heist is the metaphor; don’t underline it.
- The guards are obstacles, not villains. Comedy comes from their confusion, not cruelty.
