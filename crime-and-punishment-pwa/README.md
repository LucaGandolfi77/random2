# Crime and Punishment — A Melancholic Visual Novel

Based on Fyodor Dostoevskys Crime and Punishment. A contemplative interactive experience exploring conscience, guilt, and the weight of human choice.

> "The heat in the room was stifling. A small room with a bed, a table, and nothing more. I had been thinking... about the crime."

## Play

Become Rodion Raskolnikov. Navigate the moral labyrinth between rational theory and human conscience. Your choices determine the path — through darkness toward either isolation or redemption.

## Languages
- **English**: Default
- **Italian**: Switch via menu or settings ("Lingua: Italiano")

## How to Play

Read the melancholic dialogue that unfolds. When choices appear, select your path:
- Some choices deepen guilt and rational isolation
- Others open paths toward confession and redemption through Sonia

Your conscience, guilt, and hope levels change with each decision, shaping the ending.

## Themes
- Conscience vs rational theory
- The extraordinary man vs ordinary morality
- Guilt as the only true punishment
- Redemption through suffering and love
- The weight of an unspoken crime

## Controls
- Click choices to proceed
- ESC opens the pause/settings menu
- Auto-save after each major choice
- Save files stored locally (offline-capable)

## Files

index.html, manifest.json, service-worker.js, styles.css, icons/, src/ (config, storage, render, audio, main)

## Technical

Fully offline-capable PWA. No external dependencies. Works in any modern browser. Synthesized audio. Responsive design. Screen-reader accessible.

## Based On

Fyodor Dostoevskys Crime and Punishment (1866). Adapted with deep philosophical respect for the original texts moral and psychological exploration.

## Ending Variations

- **Isolation**: The extraordinary man walks alone, crushed by guilt without redemption
- **Justice**: Confession and punishment through legal justice
- **Redemption**: Confession through Sonias love, finding spiritual freedom through suffering

Each ending reflects a different philosophical position on guilt, justice, and human capacity for change.

A melancholic, contemplative experience for those who seek meaning in suffering, and redemption through love.
